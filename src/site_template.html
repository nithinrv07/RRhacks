      How the rankings were made

      Pressure is measured, not guessed: a win-probability model says how much each ball could change the result, and players are judged on those balls against what an average player does in the same spot.

      1. Rebuild every ballScore, wickets, balls left and target before each of 200,961 deliveries, with chase targets reconciled to official results.
      2. Predict who winsA chase model (AUC 0.90 on unseen matches) plus a projected first-innings total; team strength comes from a pre-match Elo rating.
      3. Measure leverageThe expected swing in win probability from this ball's possible outcomes, divided by an average ball's. Pressure ball = leverage of 1.5 or more.
      4. Score each ballRuns, survival, runs saved, dots, wickets and win probability added, compared with the average pressure ball in the same situation and format.
      5. Compare fairlyAt least 120 pressure balls; small samples pulled toward the average; Pakistan players excluded because they cannot enter the IPL auction.
      6. Check it holds up200 match resamples, split-half tests and ten alternative settings. Bowling ratings repeat across periods; batting ratings are less certain.
